Why private

Some industries simply cannot send customer calls to someone else's cloud.

Healthcare, banking, government and legal services handle data governed by strict residency and compliance rules — patient records, financial information, citizen data, privileged communications. For these organisations a cloud-only voice platform is disqualified before the conversation starts: the call audio, the recordings, the transcripts and the AI model itself would all sit on infrastructure they neither own nor control.

A private deployment changes that. The model runs on dedicated, single-tenant infrastructure — not shared, not multi-tenant — and the conversation data stays inside an environment you control or that is ring-fenced for you, and that you can audit. That is the difference between hoping it is compliant and being able to show exactly where every call lives.

The market, honestly

The voice-AI market is large, fast-growing and crowded. Here is the honest map of where everyone sits — and where we sit.

Cloud platforms

ElevenLabs, Vapi, Retell, Voximplant

Genuinely capable, polished products, fast to start with.

Strength: quick setup, strong voices, good developer tooling.

Trade-off: your call data runs through their cloud; per-minute pricing that grows as your volume grows; you do not control the stack — a hard fit for regulated data.

DIY frameworks

Rasa, Pipecat, self-hosted components

Private and controllable — open tools you run yourself.

Strength: full control, data stays with you, no per-minute vendor meter.

Trade-off: you get software, not a service. You architect, deploy and operate it — a steep learning curve carried by your own engineering team.
