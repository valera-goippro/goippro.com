How an AI contact center actually works

Every platform on the market is a variation of the same pipeline. There is no magic in it.

Speech-to-text
caller audio to text
→
Language model
understands & decides
→
Text-to-speech
reply back as voice

A caller speaks; speech-to-text converts the audio to text; a large language model reasons about it and decides what to say; text-to-speech turns the reply back into a natural voice — all over a live phone connection, in real time.

The whole game is latency. End-to-end response time, not voice quality in isolation, is what decides whether an agent sounds like a conversation or an uncanny delay. The agent also has to handle barge-in — the caller interrupting mid-sentence — without falling apart.

There are two ways to build it: a cascade stack, where the three stages are separate and each model can be swapped, giving maximum flexibility; and speech-to-speech, where a single model collapses the pipeline for lower latency but locks you to one vendor. Either way, the technology is an engineering pipeline — which is exactly why wiring it into a real business is the hard part.

The vendors, honestly

Four cloud platforms dominate the 2026 shortlist. They look alike on the surface and bet on different things underneath. Here is the straight read.

ElevenLabs

Cloud · voice-quality leader

The benchmark for natural-sounding voice — very low latency, thousands of voices, 70+ languages, and an enterprise push through an IBM watsonx partnership in 2026. If the headline requirement is how the agent sounds, this is the reference.

Honest note: strong on voice, thinner on operations. Reviewers describe production monitoring as limited — to the point that other companies sell add-on tools just to test and watch ElevenLabs agents.

Vapi

Cloud · provider-agnostic orchestration

Does not build its own voice engine — it stitches together 14+ speech and language providers behind one orchestration layer, so you can mix best-in-class components without lock-in. It handles around 62 million calls a month at a 99.99% SLA.

Honest note: a public Trustpilot rating near 2.6/5, with recurring user reports that platform updates break working agents without warning, and support routed through a public Discord rather than dedicated managers.

Retell

Cloud · telephony-native, compliance-focused

Built for phone-first deployments with structured dialog flows and compliance features — pay-as-you-go pricing from roughly $0.07–0.14 a minute. The pick when uptime and compliance lead the requirements.

Bland

Cloud · high-volume outbound

Purpose-built for outbound at scale — millions of calls — with high concurrency. Notably, it also offers a self-hosted and even air-gapped deployment option, which most cloud platforms do not.

The DIY layer — Rasa, Pipecat, Deepgram self-hosted

Open tooling you run yourself

Private and fully controllable — open frameworks and self-hostable components. The trade-off is ownership: you architect, deploy and operate the stack, carrying the engineering yourself.

Every one of these is genuinely capable software. The shelf is full. The question is not whether they work — it is what they all quietly have in common.

The cost model — and why it matters

At small volumes the per-minute meter is fine. At enterprise volumes it is the silent line on every invoice.

Cloud platforms — metered

The bill scales linearly with how much the system runs. Typical platform rates sit around $0.07–0.15 per minute, before language-model tokens and TTS voices are added on top.

A moderately busy support line, running through the day, easily reaches $50,000–150,000 a year in platform fees alone. The part you cannot negotiate down is the vendor margin baked into every minute.

Private deployment — fixed

Dedicated infrastructure, integration engineering, ongoing operations. Heavier upfront, but cost does not grow with call volume — it grows with capacity you choose to add.

Past a usage threshold — different per deployment, often tens of thousands of minutes a month — the per-call cost drops below the cloud line and stays there. The vendor margin, the data exposure and the lock-in stop being something you pay for.

The one thing they share

With narrow exceptions, the platforms enterprises actually shortlist are cloud-native.

That means three structural facts. First, your call audio, transcripts and recordings transit the vendor's cloud. Second, pricing is a per-minute meter that grows with every call you make. Third, you are on their stack — their updates, their roadmap, their outages.

For regulated industries — healthcare, banking, government, legal — the first fact alone is disqualifying. Patient records, financial data, citizen data and privileged communications cannot sit on infrastructure the organisation neither owns nor controls. No amount of voice quality fixes that.

A private deployment removes the structural problem at the root: the model runs on dedicated, single-tenant infrastructure, and the conversation data stays inside an environment you control and can audit.

Frequently asked, honestly answered

The questions sophisticated buyers actually ask. The answers we give.

How long does this actually take to build?

A typical engagement is eight to twelve weeks. Scoping takes the first two; integration build is the bulk; soft launch with monitoring starts around week nine. Substantially shorter usually means corners cut. Substantially longer usually means the scope grew.

What happens when the AI does not know the answer?

Hand-over to a human operator inside the same call, without dropping the customer. The trigger is configurable — confidence threshold, specific topics, explicit caller request — and the human picks up with the full conversation context. Every modern voice agent needs this; it is not optional.

Can the voice sound as good as ElevenLabs?

Yes. Voice quality at the top of the market is now effectively a commodity — multiple TTS engines deliver natural voices across dozens of languages, and the engine is a swappable component in the stack. Voice quality is no longer where deployments succeed or fail. Operations are.

Can we audit the system?

Yes. Because the deployment is dedicated to you, you can audit the model weights and prompts, the call logs, the data flow and the access controls. With a cloud platform, the most you usually get is API logs and the vendor's own assurances.

Which language models can we use?

Whichever fits the task and the compliance constraints. Open-weight models — Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, Mistral — for full data control. Hosted models — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google — when the use case allows and a vendor agreement covers it. In regulated deployments we lean toward open-weight self-hosted, for the same reason we lean toward private voice.

What about uptime?

The system is built on dedicated infrastructure with redundancy that matches the telephony standards your business already runs on. Primary and failover regions, target availability, response-time guarantees — defined with your team during scoping, because what 'uptime enough' means is genuinely different for a bank than for a marketing line.

What languages are supported?

The major European, Slavic and Latin American languages are mature out of the box — English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Polish, Ukrainian, Russian. Less-resourced languages are possible but need extra model tuning — flag them during scoping so we plan for it.

What if we want to switch providers later?

The architecture is built so each layer — the speech engine, the language model, the orchestration — is replaceable. You are not locked into a single vendor inside the stack, and in the long run, not locked into us either. Openness here is a feature, not a risk.

Before the first call

To make scoping productive, come with rough answers to these — rough is fine, not polished.

Industry, and what the calls actually do — support, sales, scheduling, screening. Languages required — primary, and any secondary. Current call volume — per day, and at peak hour. Data and compliance constraints — the regulations your industry operates under and the data classifications involved.

You will not have polished answers to all of these. That is fine — uncertainty is part of the work. But arriving with even rough sketches saves the first two weeks, and tells us we are talking to a serious team.
